Transcribe your recording archive and your recorded calls, on machines you choose.

SwarmScribe takes recordings from two places: the folders where your archive already lives, and calls your phone platform forks to it as they happen. Consent rules decide what gets in. The work is shared across every machine you connect, from servers to ordinary PCs.

In development. Not available yet.

Two ways in, both behind consent

Recording archives

Point SwarmScribe at folders in Azure Blob Storage, Google Cloud Storage or a local disk. Each folder carries a short allowlist of the recordings that may be transcribed. SwarmScribe checks for new files on a schedule, or whenever you ask, and queues only what the list allows.

  • Audio and video files, as they already sit in storage
  • Transcripts are written back beside the recordings
  • A file that changes is transcribed again

Recorded calls

Session border controllers and contact-centre platforms send calls to SwarmScribe over SIPREC, the standard for forking a copy of a call to a recorder. A call that matches none of your rules is turned away before any audio arrives.

  • Encrypted in transit: SIP over TLS and SRTP media, and unencrypted calls are refused
  • Each call stored as one stereo file, one party per side
  • Transcripts labelled by role, such as Agent and Customer, never by name or number
  • Built to grow to 100,000 calls a day by adding machines
  • Runs in the cloud or on-premises beside your own equipment

How it works

  1. Consent rules decide what gets in

    A folder's allowlist covers archive files. Call rules, matched on who sent the call and where it was headed, cover calls. Nothing else becomes a job.

  2. Machines join with one command

    A follower needs Docker or Kubernetes and a join token. It uses a GPU if it finds one and falls back to the CPU if not.

  3. Each follower takes one recording at a time

    Followers call the leader and never accept incoming connections, so a PC behind a home router works as well as a server in a rack.

  4. Results land in your storage

    Every finished transcript is stored with its recording, in the storage you already use.

What you get for every recording

Plain text

The full transcript, ready to read, search or index. When each speaker has their own channel, every line says who spoke.

Subtitles

A standard SRT file that drops straight into a video player or editor.

Word-level timing

Start and end times for every word, with the settings and vocabulary that produced them.

  "channel": 0 }

Teach it your words

Names, places and product terms are where general-purpose speech recognition stumbles. SwarmScribe lets you fix that once and apply it everywhere.

A vocabulary list
One term per line, most important first. The list steers recognition across the whole recording, and each transcript records exactly which terms were used.
Corrections
Write what it heard and what it should be, such as jay son => Jason. Fixes apply to whole words only, and the original wording is kept alongside.
A report of what to add next
SwarmScribe ranks the words it was least sure of, across all your recordings, so you can see which terms are worth adding.
Re-run what changed
After you update the vocabulary, re-queue the recordings it affects. The previous transcripts stay in place until the new ones are ready.

Built to be left running

Nothing gets stranded
If a follower dies mid-job, its recording goes back in the queue. Every recording ends up either finished or parked with a reason.
Followers hold no keys
A follower never sees your storage credentials. It gets a short-lived link to one file, and nothing more.
The leader can be restarted any time
All state lives in Postgres, so any copy of the leader can stop without losing work.
Every change has a name on it
Administrators sign in with Microsoft Entra ID or Google, each with a viewer, operator or admin role. Every change they make is recorded against them.
Your hardware, your audio
Transcription runs on open Whisper models on machines you choose. No recording is sent to a transcription service.
You can see what is happening
Queue depth, each follower's throughput and every failure are exposed as metrics, without reading logs.

Where it stands

SwarmScribe is being built now. The transcription engine and the core of the leader exist; followers and call recording are designed and come next. There is nothing to download yet, and no date to announce.

Not planned yet

  • Telling apart speakers who share one channel
  • Languages other than English
  • Translation
  • An installer that does not need Docker

Planned after the first release

These are agreed and will each be designed and built in turn. None of them is in the first release.

Redaction
Find sensitive details such as card numbers, government IDs and personal information, remove them from the transcript and silence the same moments in the stored recording. Each redaction records which rule made it. This comes before anything that lets more people see transcripts.
Search and tagging
Search every transcript and call by what was said. Phrase rules tag calls automatically, and calls can be found by agent, team, queue and date.
Live monitoring and compliance alerts
Transcribe calls while they happen, let authorised people listen in, and raise an alert the moment a required disclosure is missed or prohibited language is used. Flagged calls stay flagged for review afterwards.
A web dashboard with playback
See current and peak call volume, browse recent calls, and play a recording alongside its speaker-labelled transcript. Share a call with a link that expires, with every share recorded.
